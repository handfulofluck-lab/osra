## 2. Key verified findings

**WhatsApp/messaging.** Only Qustodio, Bark and MMGuardian claim WhatsApp; all are *chats only* (no product documents WhatsApp call monitoring). On Android it is done via AccessibilityService screen reading. Qustodio's SMS/call monitoring and call blocking require the APK from qustodio.com; the download page states "Due to new Google policies, we can no longer ask for Calls & SMS permissions when you download via Google Play." Qustodio iOS "Calls & Messages" and WhatsApp/Line require a Windows/Mac desktop app with the iPhone cabled for backup-sync. Bark says its Android AI reads WhatsApp chats and SMS but does not list calls. MMGuardian splits message monitoring into a second child app ("MMGuardian Messages"); whether that app is Play-distributed is unverified.

**Google Play policy constraints.** (a) Call Log/SMS policy: READ_SMS/READ_CALL_LOG only for the default SMS/Phone/Assistant handler; parental control is not an exception, and deriving SMS/call data through other means is prohibited — hence the off-store APKs. (b) AccessibilityService policy: monitoring apps are explicitly "non-accessibility tools" → must show a standalone prominent in-app disclosure with affirmative consent, file a Play Console declaration with video, and may not perform autonomous actions. (c) Stalkerware/isMonitoringTool: manifest `<meta-data android:name="isMonitoringTool" android:value="child_monitoring"/>` required on every build; Play Protect warns the child that a monitoring app is installed. (d) Families policy: apps *solely* targeting children may not request location — so the child-side app must be declared as targeting parents/mixed audiences, not "designed for children." Also new: location permission + Contacts policy effective 27 Jan 2027 (details unverified).

**Off-store distribution is getting harder.** Android 13+ "Restricted settings" blocks sideloaded apps from Accessibility and Device Admin until the user manually allows them per-app; Samsung Auto Blocker must be disabled on newer Galaxy devices. Google's developer-verification mandate blocks installs of unverified-developer apps on certified devices from 30 Sep 2026 in Brazil/Indonesia/Singapore/Thailand, global in 2027; unverified installs need a 24-hour "advanced flow". An off-store APK remains possible but the developer must be verified.

**Android tamper protection.** Industry pattern: Device Admin (uninstall block) + Accessibility (detect Settings/uninstall screens and overlay-block them) + parent password to remove; alert parent when either is disabled. Qustodio: if Accessibility is off, "all monitoring stops... nothing prevents the user from uninstalling." MMGuardian "Enhanced mode" locks the device if Device Admin is revoked. Samsung Knox use by these vendors: unverified. Android 16 includes a hidden OS-level "Supervision" settings page (PIN, web content filters) tied to Family Link; third-party access unverified.

**iOS limits.** Screen Time API = FamilyControls (authorization via Family Sharing; child cannot delete app without guardian) + ManagedSettings (shield apps/categories, web filter, prevent passcode changes) + DeviceActivity (schedules, thresholds, usage *reports only inside a sandboxed SwiftUI extension*). Hard limits: app selections are opaque tokens (no bundle IDs/names); usage data cannot be exported to the main app or server; extensions have no meaningful network access; no message content, no location (use CoreLocation separately), no call data; shields are minimally customizable. The Family Controls *distribution* entitlement must be requested per team; developer-forum reports in 2026 show multi-week-to-month waits with no status. MDM/supervised mode (Apple Configurator/ABM) enables real web-content-filter payloads and uninstall blocking but is impractical for consumers. iOS 26 changes: 0-minute app limits, five age tiers (4+/9+/13+/16+/18+), Declared Age Range API, PermissionKit (kids request approval to friend/chat in third-party apps), Communication Limits approvals for new contacts, Communication Safety on by default for 13–17; 26.4 requires the Screen Time PIN to revoke an app's Screen Time access.

**AI detection.** Bark: 29+ categories (bullying, predators, suicidal ideation, sexual content, drugs, etc.), scans text/image/video/audio, 60-day backfill, languages EN/ES/Afrikaans; security.org's own test flagged 97% of scenarios — Bark publishes no independent accuracy figure. Qustodio: AI-powered search/message/social alerts and "20 threat types" in SMS/iMessage; Feb 2026 Bark launched "Bark Assistant" (LLM in parent dashboard). No product surveyed advertises Arabic-language AI analysis.

**2025–26 changes.** Family Link: Feb 2025 redesign, School Time, parent-approved contacts, Wallet for kids, Google age-estimation ML. Qustodio Sep 2025: Family pause, Always-allowed apps, AI-website category, Snapchat/TikTok/X alerts. Regulation: UK OSA Protection of Children Codes in force 25 Jul 2025 with "highly effective age assurance"; EU DSA Art. 28 minors guidelines published 14 Jul 2025 (non-binding; recommend parental-control tool standards, private-by-default, age assurance); US: House passed KIDS Act (KOSA without duty of care) Jul 2026, Senate Commerce advanced full KOSA 5 Aug 2026, not reconciled.

## 3. Hard constraints for a new entrant

- **Android SMS/call content is unavailable in a Play build** unless the app is the default SMS/dialer; any such feature means a sideloaded, developer-verified APK plus Restricted-settings/Auto Blocker unblock friction.
- **Accessibility is the only route to WhatsApp/social chats on Android**; it needs `isMonitoringTool`, prominent disclosure, declaration video, no autonomous actions, and the child's device will show a Play Protect warning. Expect chats only, no calls.
- **iOS gives no message, location or call data via Screen Time**; usage data is sandboxed in extensions; app identities are opaque tokens; you need the Family Controls distribution entitlement (slow, discretionary) and the child must be in the parent's Family Sharing group. Full web filtering/uninstall protection needs supervision/MDM.
- **Location on the child app forces a non-"solely children" target-audience declaration** under Families policy; Families ads/SDK rules still apply to mixed audiences.
- **Tamper resistance depends on Device Admin + Accessibility staying enabled**; design parent alerts and a device-lock fallback; Knox-specific hardening is unverified for incumbents.
- **Web filtering realistically = local VPN or supported-browser list (Chrome/Firefox) + blocking other browsers**; HTTPS inspection is not performed by any surveyed product (domain/SNI-level only — unverified beyond vendor statements).
- **Compliance stack:** COPPA/GDPR-K, UK OSA age-assurance expectations, EU DSA guidelines, state/federal US laws in flux; Google developer verification (legal identity) is mandatory for any distribution path by 2027.
- **Market gap:** no surveyed incumbent offers Arabic-language AI alerts; Bark is US/AU/ZA-only.
