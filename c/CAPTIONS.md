# OSRA UX Contest — Option C (Assistant: "Ask OSRA" + Quick-Action Chips)

## Wave 1 Captions (`P-01-C` – `P-07-C`)

### `P-01-C.png` — Welcome / First Launch
- **What the parent does:** Reads OSRA's three plain-language promises and taps **Get started** or **Sign in** (or switches to Arabic).
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Introduces natural-language prompts right on the welcome screen so parents immediately feel relief instead of facing a complex parental-control dashboard.

### `P-02-C.png` — Sign In (with 2-Step Verification)
- **What the parent does:** Enters their password and 6-digit authenticator code to unlock the family space, with one-tap recovery chips if locked out.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Keeps 2-step security calm and guided with contextual recovery chips (**Lost your authenticator?**) right beside the code boxes instead of hidden support links.

### `P-03-C.png` — Create Account
- **What the parent does:** Enters their name, family name, email, and strong password, confirms their UAE region, and accepts OSRA's plain-language privacy promise.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Frames onboarding as a short, guided 2-minute conversation with inline password feedback and a clear privacy card rather than a wall of legal text.

### `P-04-C.png` — Add a Child + Parental Consent
- **What the parent does:** Adds Alex (12), accepts the age-smart **12–14 Teen starter rules** preset, and confirms parental/guardian consent.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Recommends sensible age-based defaults in one conversational step so parents don't have to configure dozens of limits before protecting their child.

### `P-05-C.png` — Pair the Child's Device (QR / Code)
- **What the parent does:** Scans the QR code or enters `OSR-482` on Alex's phone (or taps the computer download chip) while watching the live connection pill.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Pairs live status feedback with one-tap **Copy code** and **Ask OSRA** help chips so parents never stall if a child's device takes a moment to connect.

### `P-06-C.png` — Finish Setup on the Child's Phone
- **What the parent does:** Checks the live `4 of 5 ready` protection checklist and taps **Send reminder to Alex's phone** for the final Notifications step before tapping **Done**.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Explains in one sentence what is still missing and offers a single action chip to resolve it, replacing cryptic OS permission jargon with calm progress.

### `P-07-C.png` & `P-07-C-ar.png` — Home (All Children, English & Arabic RTL)
- **What the parent does:** Glances at the plain-language status for Alex and Sam, reviews the pending YouTube alert, triggers 1-tap actions (**Pause Sam**, **+30 min for Alex**, **Bedtime now**, **Where is Alex?**), or asks OSRA anything in the docked bar.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Surfaces the exact answers and urgent actions a parent needs in one thumb-friendly view—with full native Arabic RTL support—without hunting through multi-level tabs.

## Wave 2A Captions (`P-08-C` – `P-15-C`)

### `P-08-C.png` — Child Profile (Alex, 12)
- **What the parent does:** Checks Alex's live protection status (`Galaxy Z Flip4 · Protected`), daily summary, and triggers one-tap actions (**Pause Alex**, **+30 min**, **Ring phone**, **Where is Alex?**) or asks a question in the docked bar.

### `P-09-C.png` — Apps: Block / Allow / Limit
- **What the parent does:** Types or taps a quick chip (**Block social apps**, **Allow education**) or taps any app's status pill (YouTube `1h limit`, TikTok `Blocked`, Roblox `45m limit`, WhatsApp `Always allowed`, Minecraft `Allowed`) to adjust rules.

### `P-10-C.png` — Screen-Time Limits
- **What the parent does:** Reads Alex's progress (`1h 35m of 2h today`), compares weekday and weekend limits, and taps **+15 min**, **+30 min**, **Pause now**, or **Weekend +1h** for instant adjustments.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Turns routine limit tweaks into single-tap chips right beside a clear progress bar, eliminating modal pickers and guesswork when a parent just wants to grant 15 extra minutes.

### `P-11-C.png` — Bedtime and School Hours
- **What the parent does:** Reviews Alex's 24-hour schedule (`School 07:30–14:30 · Bedtime 21:00–07:00`), toggles either routine, or applies tonight's overrides (**Pause bedtime tonight**, **Start wind-down in 15 min**, **Allow Phone & Messages**).

### `P-12-C.png` — Web Filter and Safe Search
- **What the parent does:** Confirms Recommended protection (`13 categories blocked`) and SafeSearch ON, taps quick chips (**Allow wikipedia.org**, **Block gaming sites**), and reviews plain-language blocked summaries.

### `P-13-C.png` — Location and Map

### `P-14-C.png` — Alerts List

# OSRA UX Contest — Option C (Assistant: "Ask OSRA" + Quick-Action Chips)

## Wave 2C Captions — Child App (`C-01-C` – `C-05-C`)

### `C-01-C.png` & `C-01-C-ar.png` — Child Home (English & Arabic RTL)
- **What the child does:** Sees their remaining daily time at a glance (`45 min left today`), reads the latest parent updates (extra minutes, blocked apps, bedtime), and taps **Ask for more time**, **Ask to open an app**, **SOS**, or the docked **Ask OSRA…** helper bar.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Replaces intimidating technical permission checklists with a warm, transparent home screen—mirrored natively in Arabic RTL—so a 9-year-old always knows how much time is left and how to reach Mom or Dad.

### `C-02-C.png` — Time's Up / App Blocked
- **What the child does:** Reads the calm notice that TikTok's daily time is up until 07:00 tomorrow, sees which apps are still open, and taps **Ask a parent for more time** or jumps to **See my allowed apps**, **Open Messages**, or **SOS**.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Turns a hard lock screen into a calm, respectful pause that explains when the app returns and offers constructive next steps instead of a scary error wall.

### `C-03-C.png` — Ask a Parent for More Time or an App
- **What the child does:** Taps **+30 min** (or **+15 min** / **Unblock an app**), picks **Minecraft**, types a quick note (*"Almost finished building my house!"*), and taps **Send request**.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Guides kids through polite, structured requests in three quick taps—showing a live preview of what Mom and Dad will receive—so fewer requests end in frustration.

### `C-04-C.png` — Activity & Messages from Parents (Full Feed)
- **What the child does:** Scrolls a chronological feed of today's parent actions and notes—extra Minecraft time from Dad, Mom's dinner reminder, blocked apps, school hours, and upcoming bedtime—with one-tap **Ask for more time** and **Ask OSRA…** access.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Keeps parent-child rules transparent like a friendly conversation history, so nothing feels sudden or unexplained when an app pauses or bedtime approaches.

### `C-05-C.png` — First-Run on the Child's Phone (Pairing + Permissions)
- **What the child does:** Follows the 3-step visual tracker with the friendly OSRA shield character (`1. Scan Dad's code ✓`, `2. Turn on safety helper (2 taps)`, `3. You're all set!`) and taps **Next step**.
- **Why the Assistant model is better:** Explains OSRA's purpose in plain 9-year-old language (*"helps keep you safe online and reminds you before bedtime"*) and hides Android/iOS permission jargon behind a cheerful 3-step guide.
