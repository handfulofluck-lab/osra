OSRA privacy policy

Effective 11 November 2026.

This policy explains what the OSRA apps collect, why, where it is kept, for how long, and how to delete it. It covers the OSRA parent app and the OSRA child app distributed on Google Play. In this policy, "we" means the developer of OSRA named on our Google Play listing. You can reach us at support@osratech.com.

1. Who OSRA is for

OSRA is for parents and legal guardians who supervise their own children's phones. A parent creates the account in the OSRA app and pairs the child's phone with the OSRA child app. The child app is visible on the child's phone and shows that it is active. OSRA must not be used to monitor another adult or anyone you are not responsible for.

Accounts are for adults (18 or over). Children do not create accounts; a child's data belongs to the family account the parent created.

2. What the OSRA child app collects

The child app sends the following to the family's account, so the parent can see it in the OSRA app:

  • Location, including in the background: the phone's position, its accuracy and the time. It is used to show where the child is, to answer the parent's "locate now" request, and for alerts when the child arrives at or leaves a place the parent has set.
  • App use: how many minutes each app was used, total screen time, how many times the phone was unlocked, and which app is open while the screen is on. It is used for screen-time limits, schedules and reports.
  • Installed apps: the list of apps on the phone, so the parent can set rules per app and be told when a new app is installed.
  • Messages in chat apps the parent chooses: the text shown on screen in those apps, together with the chat partner's displayed name or number. Email addresses, phone numbers and other long numbers in the text are masked on the phone before anything is sent. Our server checks the text against safety rules (for example for bullying or self-harm). Text that raises no concern is discarded straight away and not stored. When text does raise a concern, a short excerpt (at most 240 characters) is stored encrypted so the parent can see why the alert was raised. The parent chooses which apps are checked during setup, and can switch each one off at any time.
  • Unknown contacts: if the parent has set a list of allowed contacts, a number that is not on the list raises an alert that includes that number.
  • Requests and SOS: when the child asks for more time or access to a blocked app, or presses SOS, the request or alert is sent to the parent.
  • Device and app information: the phone's model and Android version, battery level, which OSRA permissions and protections are switched on, how many other user profiles the phone has (counts only), the version of the OSRA child app, a push-notification token, and alerts when a protection is switched off. It is used to keep the protection working, to tell the parent when something needs fixing, and to detect tampering.

The child app does not read SMS or MMS messages, call logs, email, contacts, photos, videos, files or the calendar. It does not record audio. The camera is used only to scan the pairing QR code, and no image is stored or sent.

Web filtering. To apply the parent's website rules, the child app runs a local filter on the phone (Android shows it as a VPN). Web addresses are checked on the phone and are not sent to us, and we do not keep a browsing history. Name lookups are forwarded to the network's own DNS resolver; when that resolver cannot be found, they go to the public resolvers of Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) and Google (8.8.8.8), under those companies' own privacy policies.

3. What the OSRA parent app collects

  • Account details: your name, email address and password. The password is stored only as a one-way hash, never in readable form.
  • Family details: the family name, region and time zone; each child's name and birth year; the parental consent record (how and when you confirmed you are the child's parent or guardian); and the email addresses of other parents or guardians you invite.
  • Rules and places: the screen-time rules, schedules, app and website rules you set, and the places you add on the map, such as home or school (their location and name).
  • Push-notification token, if you allow notifications, so we can send you alerts.

The parent app does not read your own phone's location, contacts, messages, photos or files. It contains no advertising or analytics code.

4. How we use the data

We use the data only to provide OSRA's features to the family: showing the parent the child's location, activity and alerts; enforcing the parent's rules on the child's phone; sending alerts and emails the parent asked for (such as sign-in codes, password resets and summaries); keeping accounts secure; and fixing problems. We do not sell data, we do not use it for advertising, and we do not share it with anyone for their own purposes.

5. Service providers

We use these providers to run OSRA. They process data only on our behalf:

  • Amazon Web Services: hosting of our servers and database, in the European Union.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging: delivery of push notifications. An alert's title passes through it.
  • Google Maps: the map in the parent app.
  • Email delivery (Resend, moving to Amazon Simple Email Service): sending account emails.

6. Where the data is stored

Family data is stored on Amazon Web Services servers in the European Union, for families in every country, including the Middle East. The database is encrypted at rest. All traffic between the apps and our servers uses HTTPS.

8. Consent and the child's role

A parent cannot add a child without confirming that they are the child's parent or legal guardian and that they consent to OSRA processing the child's data. The parent can withdraw that consent in the app; the child's phone then stops sending data. Data already collected stays until it expires (section 7) or is deleted (section 9).

For the child: OSRA helps your parent keep you safe. It shows them where your phone is, how long you use apps, and warns them if something in a chat might hurt you. It does not read your texts or call history, does not look through your photos, and does not listen to you. If you are worried about anything, talk to your parent.

9. Your rights and deleting your data

You can ask us for a copy of your family's data, ask us to correct it, or ask us to delete it, by writing to support@osratech.com from the email address you sign in with.

You can delete your account yourself in the OSRA app, or follow the steps at osratech.com/delete-account. If you created the family, deleting your account deletes the whole family and every child's data. If you joined someone else's family, it deletes only your own account.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you may also complain to your data protection authority.

10. Security

All connections use HTTPS. Each paired phone signs its requests with its own key. Parents can turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app. Message excerpts are stored encrypted.

11. Changes

If we change this policy, we will update this page and its effective date. If a change affects what we collect or how we use it, we will tell parents in the app before it applies.
