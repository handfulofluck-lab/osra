OSRA privacy policy

Effective 17 November 2026.

This policy explains what the OSRA apps collect, why, who receives it, where it is kept, for how long, and what rights you have. It covers the OSRA parent app and the OSRA child app (OSRA Kids) on Android and iPhone as distributed through Google Play and Apple's App Store, and the website osratech.com. An OSRA app distributed in another way has its own notice, shown where you download it.

Who is responsible. OSRA is provided by Karim AbdelAziz, an individual acting in his own name, who decides how this data is used (the "controller" in data-protection law). No company has been formed to provide OSRA. Section 18 gives the contact details. In this policy, "OSRA", "we" and "us" mean that provider.

1. Who OSRA is for

OSRA is for parents, legal guardians and other adults legally authorised to care for a child, who supervise that child's devices. The adult creates the account in the parent app and pairs the child's device with the child app. The child app is visible on the child's device. Accounts are for adults (18 or over). Children do not create accounts; a child's profile belongs to the family account. OSRA must not be used to monitor another adult or a child the account holder has no authority over.

2. What we collect from the parent

  • Account details: your name, email address and password. The password is stored only as a one-way hash, never in readable form.
  • Family details: the family name, region and time zone; each child's name and birth year; the record of your confirmation that you are the child's parent or guardian and of each permission you give (section 9); and the email addresses of other parents or guardians you invite.
  • Rules and places: screen-time schedules, app and website rules, and places you add on the map, such as home or school (their name and location).
  • Push-notification token, if you allow notifications, so we can send you alerts.
  • Purchases: payments are handled by Apple or Google. We do not receive your card details.

The parent app does not read your own device's location, contacts, messages, photos or files. It contains no advertising or analytics code.

3. What the child app collects on Android

Depending on the features you switch on and the permissions the device allows, the child app sends the following to the family account:

  • Location, including in the background: the device's position, its accuracy and the time, to show where the child is, to answer a "locate now" request, and for alerts when the child arrives at or leaves a place you set.
  • App use: minutes of use per app, total screen time, number of unlocks, and which app is open while the screen is on, for limits, schedules and reports.
  • Installed apps: the list of apps on the device, for app rules and new-install alerts.
  • Messages in chat apps you choose: see section 4.
  • Unknown contacts: if you set a list of allowed contacts, a number not on the list raises an alert that includes that number.
  • Requests and SOS: the child's requests for more time or for a blocked app, and SOS alerts.
  • Device and app information: model and Android version, battery level, which OSRA permissions and protections are on, the number of other user profiles on the device (a count only), the child app's version, a push-notification token, and alerts when a protection is switched off. We use it to keep protection working, to tell you when something needs fixing, and to detect tampering.

The child app does not read SMS or MMS messages, call logs, email, contacts, photos, videos, files or the calendar, and does not record audio. The camera is used only to scan the pairing code; no image is stored or sent.

4. How messages are checked on Android

OSRA does not read every message or every app. It reads only the text shown on screen in the chat apps you choose, with the chat partner's displayed name or number. Before anything leaves the device, email addresses, phone numbers and other long numbers in the text are masked.

Our server then checks the text automatically against safety rules, for signs such as bullying, threats, sexual approaches or self-harm. No person at OSRA reviews a message before an alert is sent. We do not currently send messages to any AI model. Before we do, we will update this policy to name the provider and say what it keeps, and tell you in the app.

What we do with the text:

  • Text that raises no concern is discarded when the check is complete. We do not store it.
  • Text that raises a concern: we store a short excerpt, at most 240 characters, encrypted, so the parent can see why the alert was raised. The alert itself records its category and time.

Automatic checks can be wrong in both directions, and the rules catch less in some languages than in others. An alert is a prompt for the parent to look, not a finding about the child or anyone else.

The text checked can include words written by the child's friends or other people in the chat. We use their words only to check for risk to the child and to show the parent an excerpt that raised a concern, and for no other purpose.

You choose which apps are checked, and can switch each one off at any time.

5. Web filtering on Android

To apply your website rules, the child app runs a filter on the device, which Android shows as a VPN. Web addresses are checked on the device and are not sent to us, and we do not keep a record of the websites your child visits. If we later offer a report of the website names the device connects to, it will be off until you switch it on, and we will update this policy before it is offered.

Name lookups. The filter forwards name lookups to the network's own name server. When that cannot be found, lookups go to the public name servers of Cloudflare (1.1.1.1) or Google (8.8.8.8), under those companies' own privacy terms.

6. What the child app collects on iPhone

On an iPhone the child app works through Apple's Screen Time framework and collects less. It sends: location, including in the background, for the same purposes as on Android; that a group of apps you chose was blocked, and the child's requests for access; and the iPhone's model and iOS version, which OSRA permissions are on, the app version, and a push-notification token.

On an iPhone the child app does not read chat messages or anything else on the screen, does not filter or see web traffic, and does not report minutes of use per app. Apple does not give OSRA the names of the apps on the iPhone: you choose groups of apps on the iPhone itself, and we receive only the names you give those groups. It does not read SMS, call logs, email, contacts, photos, files or the calendar, and does not record audio.

7. How we use the data

We use the data only to provide OSRA to the family: to show the parent the child's location, activity and alerts; to apply the parent's rules on the child's device; to send the alerts and emails the parent asked for, such as sign-in codes, password resets and summaries; to keep accounts and devices secure; to fix faults; and to meet our legal obligations.

No advertising. OSRA shows no advertising and contains no advertising networks or advertising code. We do not use your family's or your child's data, or anything about your child's activity or alerts, to select or target advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not share it with anyone for advertising or for their own purposes.

No AI training. We do not use your family's data to train AI models. If we ever offer an optional programme to help improve OSRA's alerts, it will be off unless you switch it on, separate from our terms, available only where the law allows it, and described here before it starts.

8. Who receives data (service providers)

We use these providers. They receive only what their task needs.

  • Amazon Web Services: our servers, database and backups, in Frankfurt, Germany, in the European Union.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging, and Apple Push Notification service for iPhones: delivery of push notifications. An alert's title passes through them.
  • Google Maps: the map in the parent app. The parent's device asks Google for the map of the area shown, under Google's own terms.
  • Email delivery (Resend, moving to Amazon Simple Email Service): sign-in codes, password resets and the summaries you choose.
  • Name servers (Cloudflare, Google): see section 5.
  • Apple and Google app stores: purchases and subscriptions, under their own terms.

We may also disclose data where section 15 applies.

9. Consent and the child's role

A parent cannot add a child without confirming that they are the child's parent or legal guardian, or otherwise legally authorised, and agreeing that OSRA may process the child's data for the features they switch on, including the message checks in section 4. Each confirmation is recorded with the time. The parent can withdraw it in the app; the child's device then stops sending data. Data already collected is kept until it expires (section 11) or is deleted (section 12).

For the child. OSRA helps your parent keep you safer. Your parent can see where your phone is and how long you use apps. On an Android phone, OSRA checks the words in the chat apps your parent chose for signs that something might hurt you. A computer does the checking, and it can make mistakes. On an iPhone, OSRA cannot read any chat. OSRA does not read your text messages or call history, does not look through your photos, and does not listen to you. If you are worried about anything, talk to your parent.

10. Where data is stored and who can reach it

Family data is stored on Amazon Web Services in the European Union, for families in every country. The database and message excerpts are encrypted at rest, and all traffic between the apps and our servers uses HTTPS.

OSRA is operated from the United Arab Emirates. The people who operate OSRA can reach the systems in the European Union from there to run and support the Service, and access is limited to them. The push, map, email and name-server providers in section 8 may handle the limited data they receive in other countries, including the United States, under their own data-protection terms.

These are the periods our systems are set to apply. We check them across our systems, and if we find a copy kept longer, we correct it or update this table.

Backups. We keep encrypted database backups to recover from failures. Data deleted from our live systems can remain in a backup until that backup expires, 14 days later, and is not restored into live use.

12. Your rights and deleting your data

Depending on the law that applies to you, you can ask us for a copy of your family's data (in a form you can take elsewhere), to correct it, to delete it, to restrict or object to a use of it, or to withdraw a consent. Write to support@osratech.com from the email address you sign in with; we may ask you to confirm your identity. We answer within one month, or sooner where the law requires. Withdrawing a consent does not undo what was lawfully done before.

A parent can exercise these rights for a child within the parent's legal authority. A child may also have rights of their own. Where a request would reveal another person's information, such as a friend's words in an excerpt, we take their rights into account before answering.

You can delete your account in the parent app or by following the steps at osratech.com/delete-account. If you created the family, this deletes the whole family and every child's data; if you joined someone else's family, it deletes only your own account.

You can complain to the data-protection authority of your country.

13. Security

All connections use HTTPS. Each paired device signs its requests with its own key. Parents can turn on two-step sign-in with an authenticator app. Message excerpts are stored encrypted, and access to production systems is limited to the people who operate OSRA. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach affects your family's data, we will tell you and the authorities as the law requires.

14. Children's privacy

OSRA is a tool for parents. It processes children's data only for the features the parent switches on, does not show advertising, does not build profiles for marketing, and does not sell or share children's data for anyone else's purposes. Some features may be unavailable in some countries or for some ages.

15. When the law requires us to disclose or report

We disclose data to a court, the police or another authority only when the law requires it or a valid legal process obliges us, and only the data required. Where the law places a duty on us to report content or a risk to a child, such as child sexual abuse material, we follow that duty. Parents may have their own duties under the law of their country.

16. Changes to this policy

We publish the current version and its effective date, and tell parents in the app before a material change applies. We will not start a new use of data, or a new kind of collection, on the strength of a policy change alone: where the law requires consent or another step, we complete it first.

17. If OSRA moves to a company

OSRA may later be provided by a company. If so, that company will become the controller only through a lawful process, and we will tell you before it happens.
